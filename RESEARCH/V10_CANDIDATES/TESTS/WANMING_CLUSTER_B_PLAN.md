## Positive tests

### B-P1 — Success changes outside estimates
A small public commercial/logistics success becomes visible. Different outsiders know different pieces of information. Test whether merchants, local intermediaries and officials update behavior without receiving secret/author knowledge, and whether at least one update is wrong.

### B-P2 — Success makes an old power arrangement dangerous
An early low-value resource is centrally allocated for speed. Later the same resource becomes scarce/valuable and complaints appear. Test whether the story can identify the changed incentive and perform minimum power separation with a real new cost.

### B-P3 — Reform creates politics from concrete losses
A new arrangement removes some approval/information/intermediation power from existing actors. Test whether resistance emerges from lost money/status/control while the affected group internally splits instead of becoming a single villain bloc.

## Boundary tests

- B-B1 opponent cannot use secret information
- B-B2 learning != perfect prediction
- B-B3 no resource value change → no automatic power split
- B-B4 unclear original authority → return to power-contract rather than pretend rebalancing
- B-B5 no concrete loss → no structural-conflict explanation
- B-B6 affected group must internally differentiate

## Anti-pattern stress B-X1

Failure if:
- all opponents instantly identify Chen as the central threat;
- every old-interest character is corrupt or stupid;
- reform resistance disappears once “better rules” are explained;
- power split is costless;
- success makes the world more obedient instead of more reactive.
